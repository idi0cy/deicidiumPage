DIMENSIONALITY
"What is where, what is when, what is what, what is first?"

I'm not quite sure how well you'll be able to wrap your head around dimensionality, so I'm going to assume that you'll probably be fine, because things start getting a bit goofy.

Dimensions, we've all heard of them. I'm not talking about some alternate universe garbage, I'm talking about the dimensions you occupy right now. Height, length, depth, all of that stuff. Dimensions in this world work in similar ways, but also completely different at the same time so let's not waste any. There are three dimensions pertinent to the worldbuilding of this verse. These dimensions allow things to exist, at all, and all things must exist within these pre-set dimensions if they want to well, exist. That is except for one cluster of things that transcends dimensions (the singularity).

The first type of dimensions is the most basic one, the spatial dimension. Spatial dimensions are the easiest to interpret, because they are relatively simple and intuitive compared to the others. You move around in spatial dimensions every day. An object that extends to one spatial dimension is a line, two spatial dimensions and it's a square, and three spatial dimensions it's a cube. Simple stuff, really. You just keep adding another axis which matter can occupy and take form in.

Things get a bit tricky, because here you are allowed to take up dimensions that extend towards infinity. When you go from 3D to 4D, it's like going from a square to a cube. You're adding another dimension, or "direction" the shape goes in where one of a lower dimensionality couldn't. A fourth dimensional object spatially would have so much more mass than a 3D object. Traditionally, it would be more than infinitely more (no matter how much infinitely thin squares you stack, they'll never have the same mass as a cube), but you can actually get up the dimensional ladder if you just spend a crazy amount of energy to convert particles into versions that extend into higher dimensions.

Want a more intuitive (and gruesome) sense of what a fourth dimensional object might actually mean? I might not be able to help you, but I'll try. Imagine you are a 3D observer, looking at a 2D life form. For the sake of argument, this lifeform is like the diagram of a human's internal organs. To the 2D creature, it can't see inside of another 2D creature because the dimension it's viewing it from is blocked by the other creature. However, this 2D creature has no defence against a 3D creature, who can view it from above and see their insides. In this 2D world, the contents have no way of "spilling" out, since they are fully contained. However, a 3D creature can simply take out the insides of the 2D creature since its defences aren't present in the third dimension. For a 4D creature, they could just pluck your heart out with ease because you have nothing to stop it in the higher dimension, since you are tis but a 3D being. They can also see all your insides. Rules and effects like these apply to even higher dimensions.

Here are some clarifying details. When an object occupies a certain amount of spatial dimensions, that means it can travel in those dimensions. When it extends to those amount of spatial dimensions, that means that it's form, or the dimensional equivalent of its volume is scaled up to that amount. Example time. A 2D sheet can occupy three dimensions, since you can have it float around in 3D even though it's only really 2D. A 3D cube though actually extends to three dimensions, because it doesn't just travel there it has mass allocated across three dimensions.

Dimensions are not arbitrarily named in this world. Here, you can call the first dimension length, or height, or depth. In this world, the first dimensions is always the first dimension, and the second the second, and so on.

There is no known limit to how much spatial dimensions something can occupy.

Spatial dimensions are accessed in a couple ways. If a particle is imbued with enough energy, it can expend it to convert itself so that it is a particle that can extend to an extra dimension. This energy requirement gets exponentially larger the more dimensions you want to occupy. Particles typically are unable to go to dimensions they don't extend to, they are gatekept in a way from it. You can bypass this with a sustained energy field and some funky physics. I'm not talking about that technology in this section.

Spatial yadda yadda, who cares? Let's talk about time. Temporal dimensions represent how time moves. Now, obviously ALL of this is fictional or else I wouldn't even be talking about it, but these dimensions are much more wack in interpretation than spatial dimensions. Temporal dimensions are the amount of directions that time can flow in. For our universe (as far as we can tell) there is one temporal dimension.

What in the world does all that mean? It means that time changes one way. Moving from one time to another means that the current state of things changes to a new state of things based off a set of rules that determine how it changes. A temporal dimension is one set of rules. We only have one set of physics we constantly follow, so we are only moving in one temporal dimension. When you get two temporal dimensions, things get a lot trickier.

Imagine a 2D graph, cartesian coordinates, all that jam. You have a line function, and boom. That's how time flows. If it's a straight line, how states change are impacted differently by one set of rules than the other depending on the slope of the line. If it's a squiggly line, then one second you're following one set of rules, and the next you're following a different one. Time gets beyond exponentially more complicated when factoring multiple temporal dimensions, and that's why it almost never appears, with the exception of the fractal membrane and maybe some weird stuff near the singularity.

If you're a bit of a nerd, you'd realize that any straight line in any amount of temporal dimensions can be expressed as one ruleset since it is a constant. Well good job, because when I'm talking about two dimensional time it only really applies when sometimes it follows one ruleset and the other time it follows the other. Any linear ruleset can be abstracted into hundreds of dimensions, but the nonlinear one is the bear that has all the scientists huddled together in a campfire telling ghost stories.

There is one type of line that time will NEVER go in. Backwards. Time is intrinsically inviolable, and subconscious complex F will not allow for any structure in reality to travel backwards in time. For good reason, too.

Unlike spatial dimensions where technology or energy can manipulate the dimensionality of things, temporal dimensions do not do that. You can't just add more, it isn't possible to mess with reality on that scale (kinda).

Finally, there is the very abstract ideological dimension. Read up on the noosphere, and you'll realize that everything has an idea before it can exist. Where do these ideas sit? In an ideological dimension, of course. You need a spatial dimension to exist in a space, and ideas need an ideological dimension to exist ideologically in the same way. That's really all you need to know. I mean, there's some funky thing where ideas scale in complexity the more dimensions they have, but that's not really pertinent.

So those are all the important dimensions you need to know.
