UNIVERSE
"This sky was built for you and me"

The basic universe is exactly what it sounds like, a basic universe. Of course, there are distinctions to be made that I would like to clarify. It begins with the fact that the technical terminology for a universe is not actually "universe." The official name is referred to as the "pseudoverse," since it doesn't actually contain all of existence, but most cultures already established the term from back in the days of primitive science, so just imagine those two terms as interchangeable. Obviously, there are many universes. An infinite number in the fractalverse, in fact. You either already know about it, or can find out whenever you want, so go do whatever.

Each universe has a certain size. We are used to having our universe be around 50 billion light years (that measurement isn't used in this world) in radius. There is one universe roughly half that size, but that's only discussed in the fractalverse article. The basic universe you'll find most often dotted around the multiverse is measured by the amount of stars in it. Each star counts as a solar system, and groups of solar systems in universes are typically grouped into solar networks, as most universes are not large enough to form mature galaxies. The average universe contains at least five dozen solar systems (smallest stable universe has 27 recorded solar systems), and at the high end two hundred solar systems (largest stable universe has 243 recorded solar systems). The largest (standard) universe ever found had 243 stars, which is where the Starsplitter got its unit designation.

Additionally, the laws of physics are different in this world, so they cause some implications for how universes are formed. First of all, subconscious complex D pushes solar networks closer together for the principle of action, so the average distance between stars is only 1-3 light years (mean of 2). Further more, the principle of action also effects how much planets form, increasing their number. Emergent properties of life have a development rate much higher than is to be expected in our universe. Again, all of this is principle of action instigated.

Universes have sizes, which means that they have borders too. All universes have an unambiguous border made of a material referred to as the universal membrane (in some cultures the "firmament"). This membrane at first seems to break all laws of physics, like how light is both a particle and a wave. People just didn't see the full picture in the beginning. It has enough energy contained in it to manifest into a black hole or five hundred, so maybe keep the children away. It is impenetrable. Any attempts to pierce through it and break the membrane fail. Any attempts to permanently warp the membrane only result in it returning to its original state nearly immediately after force is no longer applied. All force put against it is met with exponentially more as the membrane deforms. At one point, a civilization managed to push it a couple thousand kilometers, so the membrane pinched the section they deformed off and formed a new membrane, leaving the machinery to float away.

Firmaments have a fairly distinct visual aesthetic. The average firmament has a smooth texture that undulates at low frequencies with low altitudes. When small forces interact with the firmament, it will respond by having smooth ripples similar to disturbing fabric. The sharper the force you apply, the less you will be able to ripple the firmament. The firmament itself has a colour mainly consisting of blue or purple, and this colour consistency shifts relative to the physical structure of the firmament.

The universal membrane is made of material occupying five dimensions (four spatial, one temporal). This means that the matter it has exceeds anything less than 4d, and even the most powerful civilizations need to devote an insane amount of materials to go into 4d. The universal membrane is an expression of subconscious complex F, but more limited than stronger expressions such as the singularity or fractal membrane. Despite its seemingly unlimited pool of energy, the universal membrane is limited. It's measured in energy units that would make Dyson spheres look like double A's, but it is limited. This limited energy is what restricts the size of universes. Branch-off universes and the central universe are able to exceed this limit because they are more closely related to the singularity (and therefor subconscious complex F) in some way, either because they merge with a fractal membrane, or are closer to the singularity itself.

Universes can grow and shrink, and can be born and die. Universal membranes don't store energy in the traditional sense, they store energy through action (principle of action, action). This action is converted into raw energy by the subconscious complex F through the universal membrane. When the universe creates more action, the universe membrane can expand. It doesn't just add more space, it also adds more matter as energy is from the universal membrane is converted into matter as well. When universes expand, more stars can form.

Because they can expand, universes can also die. When less interesting things are happening (less action), the universe shrinks back to conserve energy so it doesn't get stretched thin. Any matter in its way gets reabsorbed. After two dozen solar systems or less remain, the universe is no longer action sufficient and in a matter of hours shrinks down into nothing. This death event expels massive amounts of energy into the fractalverse vacuum. Universes can live for billions of years without dying like this, but fluctuations in membrane size can occur over centuries, or sometimes even just decades.

Universes can also form again. When enough energy gathers in one spot (maybe a quasar, energy storm, cyclone, anything of the like), subconscious complexes F and D use their influence to forge a new universe membrane there. It starts off as nothing more than an orb the size of a golf ball, but grows rapidly over the course of hours to assume the size of a regular universe. When they grow, energy from outside is absorbed into the universe and is converted into various structures (from nothing but dust to pre-built stars) when inside the universe. This matter/energy absorption ONLY happens when a universe is born, and after it is self sufficient it loses the ability to assimilate energy. Harvesting energy in large storms can be dangerous if a universe is born there.

Universe membranes usually try to maintain a five meter thickness, though at times of action famine it can shrink to three meters and in an action surplus it can grow to ten meters thick.

If universal membranes are so inviolable, how come every civilization north of ten members can go bouncing around the place? Well, there is a secret cheat code to get through the universal membrane. Each universe membrane has a certain property, allowing you to slip through it. In order for the membrane to function, it needs to know whether or not the matter surrounding it is part of that same universal membrane. In order to do this, universal membranes have an intrinsic "pattern" to them that they resonate with and bind to. Luckily, unlike the fractal membrane this pattern isn't practically infinite in complexity so civilizations won't have to pull some VERY funky technology to get through it. If you want know more about universe based transportation technology, check out the technology tab. That's the one that contains all the technology.

When universes first start forming, particles arrange themselves into distinct, universe specific patterns that are created and recognized by subconscious complex F as permission to create a universe. This marks those molecules as part of the membrane, and they are included. After a universe is fully formed however, matter is no longer assimilated into part of the membrane. Ships with dedicated compartments on their sides that have particles which can align to these patterns are able to claim they are a part of the membrane, and sort of make a hole in in that the rest of the ship can go through. Ports can also be made this way for other ships that don't have this feature, but ports are unstable since universe membranes move around.

In politics, most civilizations will gatekeep the actual universe membrane patterns/frequencies, for many good reasons. Delay invasions, prevent molestation of borders, prevent smuggling, and all that jazz. Uhh, read more on that later if you really want to.
